## How the measurement works

New in this recording, and the part the exec team will judge [00:02:33]:

- **Baseline:** four weeks of Open's existing outbound campaign, matched by time of day and by agent, *"so we are not comparing a Monday morning against a Friday afternoon"* [00:02:42]–[00:02:49].
- **Concurrent control:** branded identity on half the volume, the other half left exactly as today, so the control runs at the same time rather than last month — because answer rates move with the season and a before/after would credit Aged Ventures for things it did not cause [00:02:55]–[00:03:15].
- **Randomisation:** split at the number level, with a confidence interval reported rather than a single figure [00:03:28]–[00:03:37].
- **Time to signal:** about three weeks on Open's volumes; below that the interval is too wide for a budget decision [00:03:43]–[00:03:49]. Monty accepted this as fine inside a 90-day pilot [00:03:55].
- **Data afterwards:** stays on Open's infrastructure, aggregate results only, raw call records never leave the estate [00:04:02]–[00:04:09].

Monty's reaction was the useful part: his data team *"have been burned by vendors quoting uplift against a stale baseline"* [00:03:20], which is exactly what the concurrent control is designed to pre-empt.
